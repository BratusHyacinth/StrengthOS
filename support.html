Frequently Asked Questions

Getting started

What are the five tabs?

StrengthOS is organized into Dashboard (recovery, body map, and trends), Workout (live logging, templates, and history), Stretch (guided mobility routines), Meals (macro and calorie tracking), and Settings. Body measurements are reached from the Dashboard.

How do I import a workout?

Open the Workout tab and tap Import, then paste your workout text — or use the Share Sheet in another app (such as Strong or Hevy) to send a workout straight to StrengthOS. The app auto-detects the format and shows a preview before saving.

Which apps can I import from?

StrengthOS supports Strong (legacy and modern formats), Hevy, and any generic plain-text "weight × reps" or CSV export. If your app isn't recognized, email us a sample export and we'll add support.

Can I start a workout without opening the app?

Yes. The "Start Workout" shortcut can be triggered from the iPhone Action Button, Siri, or the Shortcuts app to launch or resume a session on the Workout tab.

Workouts & Live Activity

What's the little pill above the tab bar?

While a workout is running, an Apple Music–style mini pill sits above the tab bar so you can browse other tabs without losing your session. The timer keeps ticking — tap the pill to jump back in, or swipe it down to collapse it.

My workout shows on the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island. How does that work?

StrengthOS uses Apple's Live Activity feature to show your workout name, current exercise, "Set N of M," a progress ring, and the rest countdown (with Skip / +30s buttons) on the Lock Screen, Dynamic Island, StandBy, and Apple Watch Smart Stack. It's delivered entirely on-device — nothing is sent to a server. You can turn it off in iOS Settings → StrengthOS → Live Activities.

How does the rest timer work?

The rest timer starts automatically between sets, with vibration, sound, and a notification when rest is up. It remembers your preferred rest length per exercise and syncs that across your devices. You can adjust defaults in Settings → Workout.

The app closed mid-workout — did I lose it?

No. The active session is saved continuously, so if the app closes mid-workout you can pick up right where you left off on next launch.

How do templates work?

Save any finished workout as a named template, then start future sessions from it in one tap. You can also repeat any of your 10 most recent sessions directly from the Workout home.

Recovery & progress

My muscle recovery percentages look off. How are they calculated?

Each of the 12 tracked muscle groups starts with a research-backed baseline window (roughly 36–96 hours), which is then adjusted by seven factors: training volume, intensity, your workout exertion, sleep, protein, overall fueling (calorie balance), and recent cardio. Every factor is optional — if data is missing it defaults to neutral. Tap any muscle to open Recovery Factors and see exactly what's slowing it down. You can also customize the baseline hours per muscle in Settings → Recovery Baselines.

Do sleep and cardio really affect my recovery score?

Yes, if you grant Apple Health access. Short or poor sleep slows recovery; recent cardio (running, cycling, stairmaster, rowing, HIIT, and more) is mapped to the muscles it fatigues and extends their recovery, fading out over time. You can turn cardio off in Settings → Apple Health.

What is the Strength Profile and how are PRs detected?

StrengthOS automatically detects weight, volume, and estimated one-rep-max records from your recent sessions. The Strength Profile (from the Dashboard) shows your peak estimated strength per muscle, which muscles are under-trained, and tap-through strength trends over 30/60/90-day and longer windows.

What does the body map show?

A front/back anatomical view that shades muscles by recovery state (This Week) or by training volume (Trends). The Trends timeframe picker drives the body map, stats, a Muscle Balance radar, and a per-muscle set breakdown together.

How do body measurements help?

From the Dashboard you can log circumference at six sites (shoulders, chest, biceps, waist, thighs, calves) in inches or centimeters and track each as a trend. If a muscle's measurement stalls or shrinks over time, the app nudges it up your "train next" list so real-world progress helps steer suggestions.

Stretching

How does the Stretch tab work?

The Stretch tab suggests what to stretch based on your recovery — dynamic stretches to warm up before training, static and PNF stretches to recover afterward — and runs a follow-along session with a built-in hold timer. You can browse the stretch library, add your own, and build or reorder routines. Every session is logged with a streak and weekly progress.

Does stretching change my recovery score?

No. Stretching reads your recovery state to plan routines and tracks your sessions, but it does not feed back into the recovery calculation.

Meals & nutrition

What can I track in the Meals tab?

Full macros — protein, carbs, fat, and calories — not just protein. Log meals by hand or pull totals from Apple Health, save favorites for one-tap quick-add, and review a full-history chart with streaks and weekly averages.

Do I have to count calories?

No. Calorie counting is always optional and off until you turn it on. The daily targets and energy-balance features are designed to be eating-disorder-friendly, with supportive (never alarming) notes if a plan looks overly aggressive. You can track protein alone, or nothing at all, and the rest of the app still works.

How do daily targets and the "Eat more / Steady / Eat less" read work?

If you turn on daily targets, the app builds a calorie and macro plan from your body stats and goal (maintain, cut, bulk, or recomp), using your real Apple Watch calorie burn when available or a formula estimate otherwise. The energy-balance read compares your average intake against what you're burning and gives a simple guide plus an expected weekly weight-change estimate.

Apple Health, sync & privacy

How do I connect Apple Health, and what does it access?

Go to Settings → Apple Health and tap Connect. With your permission the app reads heart rate, calories, workouts (strength and cardio), sleep, and dietary nutrition, and can optionally write finished workouts back to Health so it can attribute heart-rate and calorie data to them. You can grant or revoke read and write access independently in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Health → StrengthOS.

Is my Apple Health data uploaded to iCloud?

No. Apple Health data — and anything calculated from it — stays on your device only and is deliberately excluded from iCloud, per Apple's HealthKit rules. Only your training log, profile, meals, stretches, and measurements sync through your private iCloud.

My workouts aren't syncing across my devices.

Make sure iCloud is enabled in iOS Settings → [your name] → iCloud and that StrengthOS is toggled on. Confirm iCloud Sync is on in the app's Settings too. Sync can take a few minutes after a new import. Note that Apple Health data does not sync by design — only your training data does.

How do I delete all my data?

Deleting the app removes all on-device data immediately, including everything derived from Apple Health. To also remove your iCloud data, go to iOS Settings → [your name] → iCloud, find StrengthOS, and delete its data — or sign out of iCloud.

Is my data private?

Yes. StrengthOS contains no analytics, no advertising, and makes no network requests except direct iCloud sync to your own private Apple account. The developer operates no server and never sees your data. Read the full Privacy Policy for details.

Is StrengthOS really free?

Yes — every feature is available from day one. There are no paywalls, no subscriptions, and no in-app purchases.
